How to Choose the Best Mortgage Rate in 2026

When shopping for a mortgage, your interest rate can save or cost you tens of thousands of dollars over the life of your loan. Here are the key factors to consider:

1. Credit Score Matters

Lenders use your credit score to determine your interest rate. A score above 740 typically qualifies for the best rates.

2. Compare Multiple Lenders

Don't settle for the first offer. Get quotes from at least 3-5 lenders to find the best deal.

3. Consider the Loan Term

15-year mortgages have lower rates but higher monthly payments. 30-year mortgages offer lower payments but higher total interest.

5 Smart Ways to Pay Off Your Loan Faster

Paying off your loan early can save you thousands in interest. Here are proven strategies:

1. Make Extra Payments

Even an extra $50-100 per month can significantly reduce your loan term.

2. Switch to Biweekly Payments

Paying half your monthly payment every two weeks results in one extra payment per year.

3. Round Up Your Payments

Rounding up to the nearest $100 can shave years off your loan.

How Much House Can I Afford? A No-Nonsense Guide for First-Time Buyers

Here's the truth nobody tells you: the bank will approve you for way more house than you should actually buy. Just because you *can* borrow $400,000 doesn't mean you *should*. So let's break down how to figure out your real number — the one that lets you sleep at night.

Start With the 28% Rule

Most financial experts agree your monthly mortgage payment shouldn't eat up more than 28% of your gross monthly income. If you earn $6,000 a month before taxes, that's roughly $1,680 you can comfortably put toward housing.

Don't Forget the Hidden Costs

Your mortgage payment is just the beginning. Property taxes, home insurance, maintenance, and potential HOA fees can add hundreds of dollars a month. A good rule of thumb: budget an extra 1-2% of the home's value per year for upkeep alone.

Your Down Payment Changes Everything

Putting down 20% helps you avoid private mortgage insurance (PMI) — an extra fee that can cost you thousands over the life of your loan. But don't panic if you can't hit 20%; many buyers start with less and refinance later.
